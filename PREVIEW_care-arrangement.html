Choosing Your FMHC Care Arrangement: Agency, Contractor, or Direct Hire

A practical decision guide for FMHC families — compare agency, contractor, and direct-hire arrangements, and find the path that fits you and your family.

Written by Faysal El Masri, CPA | Updated June 2026

One of the first major decisions in FMHC — usually during Meeting 1 with Ontario Health atHome — is how to structure your care arrangement. You have three options: contract with a registered agency, hire a care worker as an independent contractor, or hire them directly as an employee. The choice shapes your administrative load, your effective hourly rate, and the legal relationship between you and the person providing care.

There's no universal right answer. Below is a side-by-side comparison and a closer look at each path, so you can decide what fits your family.

The Three Options at a Glance

🏢

Working with a Registered Agency

You contract with a home care agency. They employ the care workers, manage all payroll and HR, and send you monthly invoices.

Minimal administration
👤

Hiring an Independent Contractor

You find a specific caregiver who works as self-employed. They invoice you; you pay them from your FMHC account.

Consistent caregiver
📄

Hiring a Direct Employee

You become the employer of record. You register a CRA payroll account, run payroll, withhold and remit deductions, and file T4s.

Full control

A Closer Look at Each Path

🏢

Working with a Registered Agency

The simplest path administratively. The agency employs the care workers and handles all of their payroll, taxes, insurance, and HR.

The main trade-off is rate. Agencies charge an hourly rate that includes their margin, which can leave less budget room for other eligible expenses.

✓ Best suited for families who want minimal administrative load and want a turnkey arrangement.

👤

Hiring an Independent Contractor

A middle path. You find a specific caregiver who works as a self-employed contractor.

⚠️ Important: The CRA doesn't go by what your contract says — they look at the actual working relationship. CRA can reclassify a contractor as an employee retroactively.

✓ Best suited for families with a specific caregiver who genuinely meets CRA's contractor criteria.

📄

Hiring a Direct Employee

The most administratively involved path, and the one with the most control. You become the employer of record.

The benefits are full control over the working relationship and consistency of caregiver. The drawbacks are the administrative weight and real liability exposure as an employer.

✓ Best suited for families with admin capacity who want full control and are comfortable taking on employer obligations.

How to Choose

The right path depends less on what's "best in general" and more on what fits your family's situation. Three questions can help you orient.

⏰

How much administrative work can you reasonably take on?

Agencies require almost none. Contractors require some. Direct hire requires real ongoing commitment.

👥

How important is having the same caregiver every visit?

Direct hire and independent contractor arrangements give you direct control over who provides care.

🛡️

How well does the working relationship match CRA's criteria?

The CRA looks at who controls the work, who provides the tools, and who bears the risk of loss.
